# Mom Test discovery protocol: Fire TV / StoryRoute

**7 October 2026. This is an original interview plan, not completed interviews. No recruitment or messages have been sent.** Method reference: Rob Fitzpatrick's [The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/).

## Evidence discipline

Explore the person's life, recent actions, workarounds and actual purchasing decisions before showing a solution. Listen for specifics; distinguish a past incident from an opinion or future promise. Ask for a concrete next step only after discovery. The scripts below are our application of that approach, not reproduced book text.

Keep three populations separate: viewers/co-viewers, switchers/current users, and publisher buyers/operators. The viewer may have the pain while a publisher controls both integration and budget. Reddit supplies recruitment hypotheses; it does not replace these conversations.

## Sampling and recruitment plan

Start with 6–8 viewers/co-viewers who have recently altered viewing because of visual discomfort, 6 actual switchers plus 3 satisfied Fire TV users, and 8–12 qualifying publisher/app-provider operators. These are proposed learning targets, not statistically representative sample sizes. Avoid recruiting only people who like the idea or only Apple fans. Seek varied Fire TV devices, providers, regions, household arrangements and current substitutes. Record no diagnoses unless necessary, voluntary and consented; do not ask anyone to demonstrate a problematic scene.

Neutral recruitment draft, for later authorized use: “We are studying how people handle interruptions, difficult scenes, and lost progress while watching television. We want to understand a recent experience and what you did about it. This is a research conversation, not a product sales pitch.” Tailor the relevant experience without announcing our proposed solution. No draft has been posted.

## Viewers and co-viewers: 25 minutes

1. Tell me about the last time a scene changed what you watched or how you watched it.
2. What were you watching, on which device and app? What happened immediately before and after?
3. What did you actually do? Who else was involved? How did you decide when to continue?
4. How did you know what you had missed? Tell me about a time that explanation was insufficient.
5. Walk me through the most recent title you decided against. What information did you check?
6. What settings, lists, equipment or other tools have you tried? What happened on the last attempt?
7. When did your current approach work well? When did you choose to accept it instead of changing anything?
8. What time, money, or coordination did you actually spend? Do not force a financial number if the burden is social or emotional.
9. Have you watched a different edit of a film before? What changed, and what did you think afterward?
10. Who selects and pays for the service or device in your household?

Follow the person's account. Ask “Can you walk me through that?” rather than steering toward flashes, summaries, or our preferred feature. Do not confuse broad photosensitivity, migraine, sensory discomfort and epilepsy, or infer one from another. Do not offer treatment advice.

After the discovery account, a harmless prototype may separately compare original viewing, manual skip, skip plus text context, and a reviewed alternate scene. Ask the participant to explain what happened in the story and what each control means. Measure task comprehension and navigation, not seizure reduction. Counterbalance order; avoid showing the solution before collecting past behavior.

Potential advancement: a second session using harmless material, or permission to observe a non-sensitive workflow. Agreement to help is research access, not willingness to pay. Respect refusal without interpreting it as a negative medical outcome.

## Switchers and current Fire TV users: 20 minutes

1. What streaming box or TV did you use before, and what do you use now?
2. Tell me about the last purchase or replacement. When did it happen and what did you spend?
3. What specific incident occurred before you decided? What else influenced it?
4. What did you try before replacing it? What worked, even temporarily?
5. What did you expect to improve? What actually improved after switching?
6. Which problems followed you to the new device? Which app or television is involved?
7. What do you still use Fire TV for? What would you miss if you removed it?
8. Tell me about the last time you considered changing but stayed. Why then?
9. Who else shares the TV and how did the change affect them?

Separate completed purchases from “thinking of buying.” Capture hardware age, providers, display/audio setup and Apple ecosystem changes as confounders. A switch establishes spending on a device, not demand for our SDK or subscription.

Potential advancement: consent to a follow-up walkthrough or a receipt with identifying details removed. Never require private viewing histories or account credentials.

## Publishers, app providers and operators: 30–40 minutes

Qualify first: do they own or control a Fire TV app, hold editing/distribution rights, have recurring releases and know the review/budget owner? A platform-supplied branded app may need its provider's engineering permission.

1. Walk me through the last title released to Fire TV, from approved master to the files viewers received.
2. What accessibility or visual-content concern actually changed that release? Who raised it?
3. What did you do? Show a non-confidential sample of the task or artifact if permitted.
4. Which existing QA, correction, warning and editorial tools were used? Why were they sufficient or insufficient?
5. Tell me about the last revised cut, subtitle change, ad change, or new rendition. What had to be checked again?
6. Who owned approval and who could change the app? When was engineering available?
7. How much staff time and external expense did that incident consume? Ask for measured records or ranges rather than manufacturing an estimate.
8. When have you chosen to leave a warning instead of editing? What tradeoff drove that?
9. Have you made alternate scenes or cuts before? What rights, editorial objections and production cost arose?
10. What is the last accessibility or playback tool you bought? Who approved it, from which budget, and how long did it take?
11. Which customer/support evidence caused an actual product change or expenditure?
12. What work would still remain if your current vendor handled this perfectly?

Ask explicitly why normal correction, a provider feature, or an internal playlist would be cheaper. A friendly advocate without authority is not a buyer. Do not ask “Would you pay for our AI shield?” or “Is safety important?”

Only after discovery, offer a fixed-scope proposal with named deliverables, rights boundaries, operator effort, support limits and a cost based on measured work. Seek a named owner, sample authorized content, and an engineering/review window. These are stronger than praise but are not a sale. Purchasing evidence is an accepted paid scope or equivalent real procurement commitment; do not label an unpaid LOI as revenue.

## Recording template

Use anonymous ID, role, recruitment source, interview date and consent scope. Record the incident date, device/app context, action taken, workaround outcome, observed expenditure/time, decision owner, contradictory evidence and next-step status. Label direct observation versus recalled self-report versus interviewer inference. Preserve a short paraphrase; avoid publishing usernames, diagnoses, recordings or private transcripts. Record the reason a hypothesis weakens, not just supporting anecdotes.

## Proposed gates and stop conditions

These are founder decision rules, not scientific validation thresholds. Revisit them if evidence changes.

- Problem: at least four independent viewer/co-viewer accounts describe recent repeated assistance, avoidance or loss of story, including some whose current substitute works well enough to challenge us.
- Preference: harmless task comparison shows whether alternate footage adds useful comprehension over skip plus context. If users prefer an existing method, change the product rather than arguing with them.
- Buyer: two independent qualified operators demonstrate recurring work that existing tools leave costly, and one commits content access, a named owner and an engineering window. Require a paid scoped pilot before claiming purchasing validation.
- Delivery: exact route/release checks and output observations pass on one selected Fire TV target. Any exposed excluded marker, stale-route acceptance or wrong-profile recovery blocks the corresponding claim.
- Economics: measured content review/edit costs and integration effort fit the buyer's demonstrated budget. Stop the standalone thesis if each account becomes unrepeatable custom production or no owner can pay.

Do not combine interview counts, Reddit votes and prospect enthusiasm into a numerical “validated” score. Leave unsatisfied gates open.
